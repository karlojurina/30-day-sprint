# EcomTalent — Design Context

This file is the source of truth for every design decision on the student
side of the EcomTalent platform. Read it before any design work so nothing
drifts toward generic AI / generic SaaS output. **If you are about to design
something that contradicts this file, update the file first, then design.**

*Rewritten 2026-09-24 as the output of the art-direction conversation
(Lovro + Fable). It replaces the "explorer's sea chart" direction wholesale.
That direction produced the dashboard Lovro described as "underwhelming and
low effort"; the thing he reacted to — "it gave me a vision" — is the sunset
world in `_admin/research/world-gen/`. The world is now the source, and the
interface derives from the painting rather than competing with it.*

---

## Design Context

### Users

Paid EcomTalent members. They bought a $97/month seat on Whop to learn how to
create ads that get brands paid, and to land real paid work. Age skews 18–30,
mixed technical skill, global (65.7% EU/NA). They open this during focused
work blocks — usually at a desk, often in the evening. **Desktop-first.**
Phones get a simpler list view that shares every piece of data and never
loads the 3D world.

The single job this interface exists to do: **tell the student exactly what
to do next, make them feel they're investing in themselves, and respect their
time on the way.**

### Brand Personality

Three words from Karlo (the founder): **locked in, valuable, chill.**

- **Locked in** — disciplined, intentional, focused. No bouncing, no
  distractions. The student is here to do work.
- **Valuable** — premium. Worth the $97. Not budget, not free-tier.
- **Chill** — confident enough not to shout. Never frantic. The interface has
  nothing to prove.

Voice on copy: direct, mentor-like, no fluff. Karlo is the voice throughout.
Contractions always. No exclamation marks unless he'd genuinely yell the
line. No "Welcome back, champion!" — no LinkedIn energy.

## Aesthetic Direction — DECIDED 2026-09-24

**The world is the product.** A flat-colour, hazed, sunset landscape in the
Firewatch idiom, script-generated in Blender (`ridge.py`), rendered in the
browser with three.js. The student scrolls *into* it. Eight places ascend from
a shore to a summit; each holds one module of the course. Everything the
student sees on the student side — every panel, badge, button, list — is
derived from that painting's palette, so a floating panel over the world
looks like it belongs in the same evening.

References Lovro named: **Gran Turismo 7's world map** (the screen-select
feel; it is not shot wide) and **Firewatch** (flat colour + haze; the whole
generator is built around it).

This is **premium, not arcade.** No neon, no pixel art, no mascots, no
gamification stickers, no confetti emoji. A student in their 20s who aspires
to make ads should feel inside something as well-made as the ads they want
to make.

### Locked, do not relitigate

- **Two levels.** The world holds AREAS. Areas hold lessons. No lesson pins on
  the world. The landmark silhouettes ARE the area markers.
- **Scroll travels in and out.** Lovro: "scroll feels perfect." Fixed canvas,
  tall spacer, camera reads `scrollY` every frame. Do not redesign this.
- **Sunset is the only hour.** No clock, no time-of-day change. One painting.
  (`TIME_TABLE` in `ridge.py` still carries dawn/midday/night; they are
  unused and stay unused.)
- **An area is the same terrain, camera parked at its landmark, panel beside
  it.** Not eight separate scenes. There is no 3D artist and no budget for
  one, and the world staying behind you while you read is the point.
- **Markers pin to their landmarks**, not to the rail's centre line. A column
  of pills floating over a landscape defeats the point of having places.
- **No new glow.** Only the lighthouse lamp (in 3D) and the current-area
  marker (in UI) may emit light. Everything else is lit, never glowing —
  see the emissive trap in the world-gen README.

## The eight places

Ascending from shore to summit. The **order is the teaching order** and is
confirmed. Terrain character and landmark are visual only; the names shown to
students are the plain module names (Lovro rejects metaphor names on sight —
his audiences scan). The landmark word may appear as a small eyebrow for
navigation-by-memory ("the viaduct one"); the terrain word never appears in
the UI.

| # | Module (shown) | Terrain (internal) | Landmark | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction | the shore | Lighthouse | exists — its lamp is one of two things allowed to glow |
| 2 | Fundamentals | the lowland wood | Windmill | exists |
| 3 | Video Ads I | the river valley | Stone bridge | exists |
| 4 | Video Ads II | the gorge upstream | **Viaduct** | **NEW** — many arches; "the bigger bridge", which is what the second half of the same topic is |
| 5 | Static Ads | the basin | Watchtower | exists |
| 6 | AI Ads & Content | the highlands | Cairn | exists |
| 7 | Creative Strategy | the plateau, with the pond | **Jetty** | **NEW** — reaches into the Ad Bounty pond; a made thing, and it is where you "get access" |
| 8 | Job Board | the peaks | Obelisk | exists — the summit, the destination |

Requirements for the set: all eight must be **distinguishable from each other
at distance** in one frame. Review them together in a single Blender still
before accepting.

**The Ad Bounty pond** is the first "feature inside an area": a small water
body on the Creative Strategy plateau with the jetty into it. In the area
list it is an ordinary row carrying `feature_key = 'bounty_access'`, `type =
'setup'`, outside the progress denominator. Do not overdesign it; the jetty
is the design.

**Made things.** The world currently reads as landscape because nothing in it
was built by people. The cheapest, largest fix: **a worn path along the rail
connecting the eight landmarks** — one mesh strip, near-zero polys. Then the
two new landmarks. Huts and other buildings are deferred; do not add them
without a conversation.

**Known seam.** The sea plane's near edge crosses the frame as a hard
horizontal line at mid depth. It is inherent (the published prototype has it
identically). Fix by fading the plane's edge, not by moving it.

**Detail budget.** ≤ 1.5 MB after Draco compression. Desktop only — phones
never load the world.

## Tokens

All values live in `src/app/globals.css` as CSS variables. Tokens are the
canonical source; components never carry a literal. Role-named, never
colour-named, so the value can change without renaming anything.

Every value below is taken from the world itself (`ridge.py` `TIME_TABLE
["sunset"]` converted to sRGB, and `web_prototype.html`) or derived from it by
lifting lightness while keeping the plum-violet cast. Pure black and pure
white never appear.

**State tokens (aliases, so components ask for meaning):**

| Token | Points at |
|---|---|
| `--color-state-done` | `--color-accent` |
| `--color-state-current` | `--color-accent` — current is expressed by *treatment* (an outline, a gentle pulse), not by a different hue |
| `--color-state-locked` | `--color-ink-3` |
| `--color-state-milestone` | `--color-milestone` |

**The floating panel over the world** — the one deliberate use of
translucency in the product:

```
background: rgba(20,15,18,.80);
backdrop-filter: blur(10px);
border: 1px solid var(--color-line);
border-radius: 14px;
box-shadow: 0 30px 80px rgba(0,0,0,.5);
```

The old direction banned "glassmorphism". That ban was against glass-on-neon
SaaS. Over a painted world a translucent panel is the only way to keep the
world present while you read, and it is the prototype's own poster card.
It is allowed here and **only** here — not on cards, not on buttons, not on
the phone view.

**Legacy aliases.** `--color-gold*` in `globals.css` (which actually hold a
pearl, `#DDDDD5`) are re-pointed at `--color-accent*` so the old dashboard
keeps compiling until W15 deletes it. Do not use them in new code.

### Type

Two families. Both already in the prototype; Lovro has seen them.

| Role | Family | Weights | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display **and** body | **Bricolage Grotesque** | 400, 500, 600, 700 | It has an optical-size axis (`opsz` 12..96) — that is the whole reason one family can do both. Google Fonts, `display=swap`, with `ui-sans-serif, system-ui` fallback. |
| Labels, numbers | **JetBrains Mono** | 400, 600 | Eyebrows, kickers, durations, percentages, counts. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` wherever digits line up. |

Retired: **Inter** (the design skill already said "never plain Inter"),
**Cormorant Garamond** (the sea chart's serif).

**Scale** (reuse the app's existing rhythm where it exists; these are the
world's):

| Role | Size / line | Weight | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero ("where you are" over the world) | `clamp(21px, 3.3vw, 31px)` / 1.1 | 700 | −0.018em, `text-shadow: 0 2px 18px rgba(0,0,0,.6)` |
| Area title | 24px / 1.15 | 700 | −0.02em |
| Lesson title | 20px / 1.2 | 700 | −0.015em |
| Row | 14px / 1.35 | 500 | — |
| Body | 14–15px / 1.55 | 400 | — |
| Eyebrow / kicker (mono) | 10.5px / 1 | 600 | +0.16em, uppercase, `--color-ink-3` |
| Badge (mono) | 10px / 1 | 600 | +0.04em, uppercase |

Typography does the hierarchy; colour does state. Display vs mono tiny-caps
vs body is enough hierarchy for almost everything.

### Shape, space, motion

- Radius: panel 14, row/button 9, badge 5, pill 999. Nothing else.
- Spacing on the world side: 6 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 22. Row height 43px.
- Motion: animate `transform` and `opacity` only. Never `transition: all`.
  Easing `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` — never bounce, never elastic.
  150–250ms micro, 400–600ms moments, 800ms+ only for arrival (the auto-travel
  is 3000ms and any input cancels it). `prefers-reduced-motion` turns every
  animation into a state change.
- The camera's smoothing stays at `6.0/s`, delta-time corrected. It is the
  difference between a glide and a snap.

### State iconography

Consistency carries the place-ness. Shapes, not icons from a library:

| Thing | Shape |
|---|---|
| Watch lesson | circle (dot) — filled when done, outlined when not, half-tint when started |
| Milestone (action item) | **diamond** (rotated square), `--color-milestone` |
| Ready to watch | small right-pointing triangle, `--color-accent`. Shown **only** where true. |
| Current area (marker) | the marker's border in `--color-accent`, gentle pulse |
| Locked | outline in `--color-state-locked`, no fill |

**Never mark the negative on every row.** "Not recorded yet" on 17 of 18
rows was measured on real data and carries no information. Mark what *is*
ready; state the count once in the area header; say "not recorded yet" in
the player, where the student actually meets it.

## Two faces, one product

**Student side ships dark** — the world, and everything derived from it
above. No light mode, no theme toggle. It is an experience the student pays
to be inside.

**Admin side ships light** (`/admin/*`, scoped to `.admin-shell`). Warm cream
canvas `#EFEDE6`, white cards, hairline borders, pearl accent `#8E8E84`. The
admin is a tool Lovro and Astrid use to make decisions about real people's
access; operational density and evidence you read carefully work better on a
light face. **Nothing in this file applies to `/admin`.** Admin follows
`src/components/admin/ui.tsx`: one radius (10), spacing 8/16/24/32/48, type
11/13/20/32, colour is signal not decoration.

## Accessibility baseline

- Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. (`--color-ink` on `--color-surface-0` is ~14:1;
  `--color-ink-3` on it is ~4.6:1 — the floor for anything that must be read.)
- Focus ring: 2px `--color-line-strong`, offset 2px.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no pulses, no auto-travel tween, no flights.
- Touch targets ≥ 40px.
- Markers are real `<button>`s and lessons are real links. Screen readers get
  "Area — N of M lessons done".

## Design principles

1. **The world is the product.** Every student screen either *is* the world or
   sits over it or (on phones) echoes it. No generic SaaS patterns: no toast
   stacks, no right-side drawers, no chat bubbles, no blue progress bars.
2. **Calm confidence over urgency.** The one deadline in the product (the
   discount) is stated as mono text. Never flashes, never pulses red.
3. **Lit, not glowing.** No gradient text. No purple/cyan AI palette. No
   glow except the two permitted points.
4. **Typography does the hierarchy, colour does state.**
5. **Respect the student's time.** One clear next action. No copy that
   restates the title above it. No button that just says "Dismiss".
6. **Dark student, light admin.** Deliberate, not drift.
7. **Plain titles.** Area and lesson names are what the thing is. No metaphor,
   no riddle. The terrain words never reach the UI.

## Bans

- Bouncy / elastic easing.
- Right-side drawer modals for primary content (tried, felt thin, reverted).
- Icon libraries used generically. The shapes above, or nothing.
- Translucent panels anywhere except the one over the world.
- Sync / refresh buttons on the student side. It syncs silently.
- "Let's get started" / "You're awesome!" / "Welcome back, champion!".
- Marking the absence of something on every row.
- Neon, pixel art, mascots, confetti, stickers, trophies.

## Where things live

- `src/app/globals.css` — tokens. Canonical.
- `src/components/world/` — the world, area screen, lesson screen, player,
  and (W9) `ui.tsx` primitives: `Panel`, `Eyebrow`, `Title`, `Row`, `Badge`,
  `Button`, `ProgressLine`, `Marker`.
- `src/lib/world/scene.ts` — the three.js port. Every number in it is the
  prototype's; change them only through `ridge.py` and re-export.
- `_admin/research/world-gen/` — the generator, the prototype, the README
  with its trap log, and the art-talk agenda this file closes.
- `world-tests/` — headless Chrome renders at 1691×1278 and 3428×1230, and
  the A/B diff against the published prototype. **Render before believing.**
- The `ecomtalent-student-design` skill — where it contradicts this file,
  this file wins and the skill gets updated.

## For the build — the work this decision produces

In `ridge.py` / `export_web.py`:

1. `LEN_Y` 1500 → 2000; regrid so eight terrain zones each keep a character
   (shore / wood / river / gorge / basin / highlands / plateau / peaks).
2. Two silhouettes: **viaduct** (gorge) and **jetty** (plateau, into a new
   pond). Named nodes `LM_Viaduct*`, `LM_Jetty*` so the scene can find them.
3. **The path**: one strip mesh along the rail through all eight landmarks.
   Slightly lighter than the ground, no glow.
4. Fade the sea plane's near edge.
5. One still with all eight landmarks in frame at distance, to check they
   read apart. Then re-export `world.glb`; run `world-tests/compare.mjs`
   against the previous export.

In `scene.ts` / `WorldCanvas`:

6. Anchor each marker to its landmark node (`scene.getObjectByName`), not to
   the rail centre; park the camera *looking at* the landmark.

In the database:

7. `update regions_next set landmark_label = …, rail_at = …` for the eight
   rows — `rail_at` tuned after the export, from where the landmarks land.

In the app (W9):

8. Tokens above into `globals.css`; fonts loaded; `--color-gold*` re-pointed.
9. `components/world/ui.tsx` primitives; W7/W8 restyled onto them; zero inline
   `rgba(` left in `components/world/`.
10. Milestone rows: diamond + `--color-milestone`.

Then W13 (Draco) and W10 (the phone view, using these tokens and no world).

**Not decided, deliberately:** huts/buildings beyond the path; any celebration
visuals (W12 uses these tokens, the moments themselves are undesigned); the
exact `rail_at` values (post-export).
