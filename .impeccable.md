# EcomTalent — Design Context

This file is the source of truth for every design decision on the student
side of the EcomTalent platform. Read it before any design work so nothing
drifts toward generic AI / generic SaaS output. **If you are about to design
something that contradicts this file, update the file first, then design.**

*Rewritten 2026-09-24 as the output of the art-direction conversation
(Lovro + Fable). It replaces the "explorer's sea chart" direction wholesale.
That direction produced the dashboard Lovro described as "underwhelming and
low effort"; the thing he reacted to — "it gave me a vision" — is the sunset
world in `_admin/research/world-gen/`. The world is now the source, and the
interface derives from the painting rather than competing with it.*

---

## Design Context

### Users

Paid EcomTalent members. They bought a $97/month seat on Whop to learn how to
create ads that get brands paid, and to land real paid work. Age skews 18–30,
mixed technical skill, global (65.7% EU/NA). They open this during focused
work blocks — usually at a desk, often in the evening. **Desktop-first.**
Phones get a simpler list view that shares every piece of data and never
loads the 3D world.

The single job this interface exists to do: **tell the student exactly what
to do next, make them feel they're investing in themselves, and respect their
time on the way.**

### Brand Personality

Three words from Karlo (the founder): **locked in, valuable, chill.**

- **Locked in** — disciplined, intentional, focused. No bouncing, no
  distractions. The student is here to do work.
- **Valuable** — premium. Worth the $97. Not budget, not free-tier.
- **Chill** — confident enough not to shout. Never frantic. The interface has
  nothing to prove.

Voice on copy: direct, mentor-like, no fluff. Karlo is the voice throughout.
Contractions always. No exclamation marks unless he'd genuinely yell the
line. No "Welcome back, champion!" — no LinkedIn energy.

## Aesthetic Direction — DECIDED 2026-09-24, MAP REDESIGNED 2026-09-25

**The world is the product.** A painted, hazed landscape in the Firewatch
idiom, rendered in the browser with three.js. The student scrolls *into* it
and travels a single trail through eight places, from a misty shore to an
island in the sky, and ends with an airship flight to one more island. Each
place holds one module of the course. Every panel,
badge, button and list on the student side derives from the world's palette,
so a panel over the world looks like it belongs in the same evening.

**The look is settled; V3 (`/dev/world-v3`) is the proof.** Painted terrain,
one stylised pine family in real woods, water with ripples, layered haze,
code-built landmarks, birds and smoke. Lovro, 2026-09-25: V3 is "10 times
better" than what came before and "we are 30% of the way there". The other
70% is the map itself: V3's places were a random set of objects, and this
file now defines what they are.

References: **Firewatch** (painted flat colour, haze, silhouettes; the whole
look); **Avatar's glowing forest** for what "magical" means (teal and violet
bioluminescence; a reference, never copied); **Astra's `/dev/world-codex`**
for scroll feel and a clean interface.

**The bar for every one of the eight stops: it is a screenshot someone would
want to share.** Lovro: "I want every single piece of the map to be beautiful,
recognizable, and screenshotable... I don't want to leave any stone unturned."
The three showpieces (the waterfall dive, the Mirror Lake, the rise to the
Sky Island) set the order we build in, and every other stop meets the same bar.

This is **premium, not arcade.** No neon, no pixel art, no mascots, no
gamification stickers, no confetti emoji.

### Locked, do not relitigate

- **Two levels.** The world holds AREAS. Areas hold lessons. The landmark
  silhouettes ARE the area markers. **One lantern per lesson** stands in the
  world (Lovro, 2026-09-28, replacing "no lesson pins"): a small post lantern
  beside the trail, on one side of it, leading up to its area's landmark in
  lesson order. Watched ones are lit, the next one pulses gently, the ones
  ahead are dark glass on dark iron. They belong to the world (Firewatch),
  never to the interface: no labels, no numbers, no click targets, no
  runway or festoon look. The interface's markers stay per area.
- **Scroll travels in and out.** Fixed canvas, tall spacer, the camera follows
  the trail as the page scrolls. The rail follows the walking path with real
  sideways travel and turns, and has no speed jumps (V2's `rail.ts`).
- **Camera angle: V3's.** It tilts about 8° down at the shore and about 16°
  high up. Lovro, 2026-09-25: "It's not that deep. We can play with the camera
  angle. I currently like the camera angle in World V3." The one exception is
  the waterfall dive (place 3), where it tips steeply down and levels out.
- **Clicking a place zooms the same main-map camera in to frame its landmark,**
  and the panel slides in with that move; the student then opens a lesson from
  the panel. Lovro, 2026-09-24: *"Just zoom into the place on the main map. It's
  up to the user to click into the checkpoint."* No separate cutscene.
- **The light follows the place, not the student's progress.** Afternoon at
  the shore, darkening as the trail climbs, full night on the Sky Island, dawn
  breaking during the airship flight, sunrise at the Gate of the Sun (table
  below). Scrolling to the Mirror Lake always shows
  sunset, whoever you are. This replaces the old "sunset is the only hour".
- **The map is big.** Places sit far apart and each stop gets its own scroll
  budget. Lovro's concern: "If we make it close, that means the map is small
  and all the checkpoints are close to each other." The scroll budget per stop
  is tuned on the grey version, on his screen.
- **The Sky Island is not visible from the start.** It stays hidden behind the
  peaks and clouds until the Mirror Lake, where it first appears **reflected in
  the still water**, and the camera then tilts up to the real thing. That keeps
  the goal a reveal and lets the map stay large.
- **The Landmark column below lives in code.** The map does not read the
  database's landmark labels. Its landmark words and mesh names are in
  `src/lib/world-v6/layout.ts` (`PLACE_DEFS`: "Great Tree", `LM_GreatTree`).
  `regions_next.landmark_label` holds the first prototype's labels (migration
  v99), which only the unmounted prototype map reads through `catalog.ts`.
- **Markers pin to their landmarks**, never to the rail's centre line.
- **Glow belongs to the world at night.** Lanterns, the Glowing Forest's
  bioluminescence, the observatory, stars and the airships' lights may glow.
  The interface never glows, except the current-area marker.

## The map

**The story: a trail from the shore to the sky, ending at a gate that frames
the sunrise.** The first five places are grounded Firewatch wilderness. Two are
magical: the Glowing Forest and the Sky Island above it. Lovro: "In the sense
that I want to have a Glowing Forest and then the Sky Island, in that sense,
yes", which means magic arrives in those two places without the rest of the
world getting weirder. You arrive by boat, and for the last place you board an
airship and fly at dawn to a lone island where a stone arch faces the sunrise.

*Rewritten 2026-09-26 night for v6 (Lovro's review of v5, decisions 16 and
17 in `world_overnight_log.md`): the falls are on our side and the camera
rides down them, the mine is gone and the climb is an open air hike, the Sky
Island has a story and the sky is its hero, and the last place is the Gate of
the Sun. How each place is built: `world_art_bible_v6.md`.*

The **order is the teaching order** and is confirmed. Students see only the
plain module names; the place names below are internal and never reach the UI.
The landmark word may appear as a small eyebrow ("the lookout one").

### The eight places

**1 · Introduction — The Landing.** A small wooden dock on a misty shore, your
boat pulled up beside it, a campfire, and a tall trail signpost with eight
arms pointing up the valley: the whole course, as a sign. *Life:* gulls, the
boat rocking, campfire smoke. *Progress:* the campfire burns from the start
(decision 51 in `world_overnight_log.md`); a lamp lights at a quarter, a half
and three quarters of the way through; the signpost's lamp when you finish.

**2 · Fundamentals — Base Camp.** Tents, a supply cabin, and a fire lookout
tower on stilts at the edge of the first woods: gearing up before the real
climb, and Firewatch's own landmark. *Life:* smoke from the cabin chimney, a
flag on the lookout. *Progress:* the lookout's lamp.

**3 · Video Ads I — The Falls.** From Base Camp the
trail follows a stream on our side of the gorge. The falls themselves are
hidden until the last moment: from the shore you see only a gold mist plume
rising out of the gorge and a thin upper fall on the mountain where the stream
begins. At the lip **the camera goes over the edge with the water** (Lovro,
2026-09-26: he wanted to go down the waterfall, which works once the falls are
on our side), looking down along the falling water at the pool below. Over
the pool it **turns round to face the falls**. **The stop and its click-in
view are outside, at the pool** (Lovro, 2026-09-28; decisions 46 and 48 in
`world_overnight_log.md`). **There is no cave behind the falls any more.**
There was one until 2026-10-13: a hidden camp with a lantern and string
lights, light through the falling water rippling on the rock, a second way
out east of the falls. Lovro took the camera out of it ("the camera between
Video Ads 1 and Video Ads 2 goes into the cave for no reason"; decision 65)
and then the cave out of the world the same day ("we can drop the cave
because yes it costs loading for nothing"; decision 66). The cliff behind the
water is solid rock. Commit `0d18ea7` has the cave, working, if it is ever
wanted back.

**4 · Video Ads II — The Gorge Trail.** From the stop at the pool the camera
takes the trail to the Trail Hut (Lovro, 2026-10-13, after it had flown
there for a few hours: "we have the path but we don't take it and go in the
air"; decision 67 in `world_overnight_log.md`). It comes down to the pool's
east shore, crosses the river on the fallen log with the log underfoot,
walks the talus fan beside Video Ads II's lanterns, goes up the north wall
by a ladder as the light turns to sunset (three ladders with two plank
landings, hung on the ledge's south-west face; Lovro, the same day: "let's
just make a ladder going up the mountain just so it doesn't look like we're
flying"; decision 68), passes the hut's door and the handrail on the
ledge, and at the ledge's far end turns round to the hut and the whole
waterfall across the gorge. It faces where it is going, so the falls are
behind it from the log on and come back as the last turn's reveal. (The
other way to walk it, watching the falls the whole time, is kept in
decision 67 as rows ready to paste.) The trail on the ground starts on the
pool's east shore, among Video Ads I's lanterns, and follows the river's
bank to the log (it used to come out of the cave's side passage). On the gorge's far wall: switchbacks cut into warm lit rock, a rope
handrail on old posts, spray drifting past. Halfway up, on a wide
ledge, hikers built a small **trail hut** facing the falls: the checkpoint, with
the whole waterfall and the valley behind it in view. The trail runs on along
the cliff, up a steep gully, and **bursts onto the ridge above the Mirror
Lake** (the frame Lovro liked, kept exactly). This replaces the mine, which
Lovro found too long and too dark ("it defeats the point of what we said that
we want to have nice scenery"). *Progress:* the hut's lamp.

**5 · Static Ads — The Mirror Lake.** Out of the ravine onto a high plateau:
a wide, perfectly still lake reflecting the peaks like a photograph, with a
boathouse on a long jetty. Still water holds a still image. It is the biggest
module (25 lessons) and gets the widest, calmest place on the map. **The Sky
Island's first appearance is here, in the reflection.** *Life:* a fish breaking
the surface now and then, rings spreading across the stillness.

**6 · AI Ads & Content — The Glowing Forest.** Past the lake the trees grow
huge and ancient, and the forest starts to glow. **It is a whole forest the
camera travels through,** dense with giant trees across the whole area, never
one tree on a lawn (Lovro: "a whole weird forest thing"): teal and violet light on
ferns and mushrooms, fireflies and drifting glowing seeds, one giant tree at
the centre with a lit canopy. AI is the part of the course that feels like
magic, so this is where the world turns magical (Avatar's glowing forest as
the reference, never copied). It only reads once the light has gone down,
which is why the sun sets as you climb. The floating steps up to the Sky
Island are there for every student: each one settles into place as the camera
comes up the route toward it, whatever the student's progress (`placeSteps`
in `src/lib/world-v6/blocks.ts`).

**7 · Creative Strategy — The Sky Island.** Stone steps float up out of the
forest into the sky, drifting and bobbing, each one settling into place as
the camera approaches. **The camera sits behind the first step and looks up
along the staircase,** so you watch the stones line up ahead of you, and as
you rise it tilts up to show the island hanging above (locked: Lovro, "It's
absolutely phenomenal"). **The island is the stargazer's island:** someone came
up here to map the whole sky. One path leads from the cove where the steps
arrive to an **observatory** on a stone terrace, its door facing the path and
its telescope aimed at the stars; a spring beside it feeds two streams that
**fall off the island's edges all the way down into the clouds**; a bench at
the island's tip looks back over the whole journey below. Nothing sits on a
path and every path leads somewhere. **The sky is the hero** (Lovro: "you're
reaching for the sky. The sky is the limit"): the stop looks up at aurora in
the forest's teal and violet, a coloured Milky Way, a big moon, and **eight
constellations drawn as the eight landmarks**, each lighting a star as its
lessons are done. Clicking the observatory flies you through the telescope
into the sky. Creative strategy is seeing the whole picture, and the
observatory is only right at night. **The Ad Bounty** is a smaller island
tethered alongside, reached by a rope bridge that sways in the wind: a quest
board and a lantern. **The camera crosses the rope bridge to it** on the way out
of this area. It stays separate from the main island, as Lovro asked. In the app
the place's panel shows it as its own row under the lessons: a button that
opens its own page. It has no lantern and is in no total, because the catalog
marks it as not counting toward progress (`src/lib/world/map-model.ts`).
An airship is moored at the bounty island's far end: the way to the last
place. *Life:* shooting stars, the aurora moving, the bridge swaying.

**8 · Job Board — The Gate of the Sun.** You board the airship moored at the
bounty island and **fly across open sky** as the sun comes up: the camera rides
the ship, the clouds below turning gold (locked: Lovro, "I like the camera
movement, and I like the flying boat"). Ahead, on a lone island, stands a
**giant stone arch facing the sunrise**, the sun rising inside it. Brands pin
their job notices on its pillars. The ship docks, you walk up the causeway,
and **the last scroll stops on the arch framing the sunrise** (decision 55 in
`world_overnight_log.md`: the fly-through ended on blank sky). Getting hired is
walking through the door into your own morning. Lovro, 2026-09-26: the town
was underwhelming, and the last place has to be the most magical. *Life:*
birds flying through the arch, other airships heading out into the sunrise,
notices fluttering. *Progress:* your airship is waiting at the bounty
island's dock once Creative Strategy is done; the arch's lanterns light when
Job Board is done.

### Progress on the map

Nothing about progress is a sticker or a popup; the world itself changes.

- **One rule everywhere (2026-09-28):** a lantern lights for every lesson you
  watch, so the trail from the shore up to where you are becomes a line of
  lights, and the next one pulses where you'll walk next. Readable at a
  glance, and at its best in the night sections.
- **Places wake up, they never sleep.** Progress only ADDS light and life. A
  place you haven't reached looks exactly as it does for a new student: no
  mist, no darkened places. As an area's lessons are watched its place gains
  life in steps (about 25, 50 and 75 percent: smoke, a lamp by the door,
  lamps on the path), and the landmark's own lamp lights at 100 percent.
  Finished places stay fully lit. Progress 0 is what every student sees at
  the cutover, so it must look complete on its own.
- **The area-done moment:** when an area reaches 100 percent while the page
  is open, the light runs along that area's lanterns (about a second) and the
  landmark's lamp catches. No popup, no confetti.
- **Signature moments:** the constellations
  fill in star by star through the telescope at the Sky Island (one sign
  per place; they show only in the telescope's view, never in the open sky,
  decision 46), your
  airship waits at the bounty island's dock (7 → 8). The floating steps are
  not a progress moment: they settle as the camera approaches, for everyone.

### The progress interface (2026-09-28, Lovro's picks from options A and B)

Option C (the field journal) was dropped as too obstructive. "The current
style is best, but we need to dial it in": plum and sand, Bricolage and
JetBrains Mono, tighter.

- **The card.** When the camera rests at the student's place (after the
  arrival travel or a scroll stop), a compact card sits beside the landmark
  in the quiet part of the frame, joined to its pin by a thin leader line:
  place number and landmark word, the module name, lesson ticks ("18 of 22 ·
  4 to go"), the next lesson with its length, and **one** button, **Watch**
  (A's word; lessons play, they are never checked off). It never covers the
  landmark (a hand-tuned side per stop, with fallbacks per screen size) and
  shrinks back into the place's label as soon as the camera moves. At most
  8% of the frame.
- **The chip.** When the student's place is off screen, a compact chip on
  the bottom-left edge names it, points toward it (one click travels there)
  and keeps Watch one click away.
- **The panel.** Clicking a place (the structure or its label) opens its
  lessons in the click-in frame's quiet side, joined to the landmark by the
  same thin line: header, one-line description, ticks and counts, the watched
  lessons folded into one row with Show, the next lesson highlighted with
  Watch, the rest listed, action items as violet diamonds. Back to map and
  Esc. At most 25% of the frame.
- **The done card.** A place reaching 100% in the session gets the world's
  moment (above) and a one-line card: "Video Ads II done." and Start Static
  Ads.
- **The chrome (map review, 2026-10-15).** The header's left holds the Ecom
  University logo alone (Karlo's lockup with "| Ad Creative", white, 20 px
  tall on a computer). Its right is one pill, in this order: the streak and
  achievements (one button; the badge list opens under the pill), the 30%
  off segment with its days left, the Playbook link for a student who has
  it, and Sign out last (its glyph, the word on hover). On a phone the stats
  keep the header's row and the discount and the Playbook drop to a pill of
  their own under it. The bottom timeline carries the course's progress, in
  a capsule drawn like the header's pill: the count ("53/140 lessons"; the
  word goes on narrow screens), a
  cell per place round each of the eight diamonds, filled by that place's
  lessons, the student's diamond highlighted, a thin tick for the camera,
  and the 30% off tag on a post above the bar between the 4th and 5th
  diamonds. The old Next block and dock are gone.
- **Cheap laptops.** Card, chip and panel are the one translucent surface
  (`backdrop-filter`) only at tiers 0 and 1; at tiers 2 and 3 and in poster
  mode they are solid. Poster mode shows the card and the panel too.

**The discount checkpoint (2026-10-14).** The one offer in the product, 30%
off the next month for watching every video through Video Ads II in the
first 14 days, is a checkpoint on the trail, never a ninth place: a trail
cache on stilts on the saddle between the Trail Hut and the Boathouse
(`src/lib/world-v6/checkpoint.ts`, `dress-checkpoint.ts`), dimly lit from
day one, its lantern lit when the student is close, its ladder up, door
ajar and pennant flying when they can apply, its door open once they have.
The interface gives it one shape, a price tag on its side, in sand: a
segment in the header's pill ("30% off", then "11 days left"; once the
student can apply, a sand ground and "Apply · 9 days left"), a tag on a
post above the timeline between the 4th and 5th diamonds, and a marker on
the cache. The tag always says "30% off", never "30%" alone, which on a
progress bar reads as how far along: outlined while locked, filled sand
once the student can apply. Any of them opens its panel, the place panel's
surface, with the camera parked at the cache. The deadline is mono text
and nothing about it pulses. A student who can never earn it
(joined before the new course, or the window passed with no application)
sees none of it; the cache stays as scenery.

**Locking ships as `open`.** `WORLD_LOCK_MODE` in `src/lib/world/access.ts`
sets it. Lovro chose `open` on 2026-10-07 "for now"; the choice between open,
look and locked stays his and Karlo's. The existing docs
disagree: the `ecomtalent-student-design` skill says never lock future
lessons, and the rebuild memo says the map must be gated by progress. The grey
version supports three modes behind one switch so both can feel them:
**open** (scroll anywhere, enter anything), **look but don't enter** (see
everything, but places not yet reached sit in mist and cannot be opened),
and **locked** (the scroll ends at your frontier with a view of what's next).

### Budgets

Desktop only; phones never load the world. v6 budgets in frame time, because
pixels decide the frame rate: every stop and every frame of the route at 5 ms
or less (hard cap 6.7 ms) on Lovro's M2 Pro at pixel ratio 1.5, which comes to
about 60 fps on an ordinary Intel laptop; ≤ 600k triangles and ≤ 80 draw calls
per frame counting the lake's reflection; every shader compiled behind the
loading screen. Four quality tiers picked by the student's GPU. When idle: full
rate for 10 s, then 15 fps, then no drawing after 45 s until the next input;
never drawing while the tab is hidden. Details: `world_art_bible_v6.md`
section 3.

## Tokens

The world's values are declared as CSS variables in
`src/components/world-v6/tokens.module.css`: one `.tokens` class, carried by
the map's root and by the wrapper of every screen drawn over the map or in
its place. They stay there until they move to `src/app/globals.css`, which
today still holds the old dashboard's values under some of the same names.
Tokens are the
canonical source; components never carry a literal. Role-named, never
colour-named, so the value can change without renaming anything.

Every value below is taken from the world itself (`ridge.py` `TIME_TABLE
["sunset"]` converted to sRGB, and `web_prototype.html`) or derived from it by
lifting lightness while keeping the plum-violet cast. Pure black and pure
white never appear.

**The floating panel over the world** — the one deliberate use of
translucency in the product:

```
background: rgba(20,15,18,.80);
backdrop-filter: blur(10px);
border: 1px solid var(--color-line);
border-radius: 14px;
box-shadow: 0 30px 80px rgba(0,0,0,.5);
```

The old direction banned "glassmorphism". That ban was against glass-on-neon
SaaS. Over a painted world a translucent panel is the only way to keep the
world present while you read, and it is the prototype's own poster card.
It is allowed here and **only** here — not on cards, not on buttons, not on
the phone view.

**Legacy aliases.** `--color-gold*` in `globals.css` (which actually hold a
pearl, `#DDDDD5`) are re-pointed at `--color-accent*` so the old dashboard
keeps compiling until W15 deletes it. Do not use them in new code.

### Type

Two families. Both already in the prototype; Lovro has seen them.

| Role | Family | Weights | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display **and** body | **Bricolage Grotesque** | 400, 500, 600, 700 | It has an optical-size axis (`opsz` 12..96) — that is the whole reason one family can do both. Google Fonts, `display=swap`, with `ui-sans-serif, system-ui` fallback. |
| Labels, numbers | **JetBrains Mono** | 400, 600 | Eyebrows, kickers, durations, percentages, counts. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` wherever digits line up. |

Retired: **Inter** (the design skill already said "never plain Inter"),
**Cormorant Garamond** (the sea chart's serif).

**Scale** (reuse the app's existing rhythm where it exists; these are the
world's):

|---|---|---|---|
| Hero ("where you are" over the world) | `clamp(21px, 3.3vw, 31px)` / 1.1 | 700 | −0.018em, `text-shadow: 0 2px 18px rgba(0,0,0,.6)` |
| Area title | 24px / 1.15 | 700 | −0.02em |
| Lesson title | 20px / 1.2 | 700 | −0.015em |

Typography does the hierarchy; colour does state. Display vs mono tiny-caps
vs body is enough hierarchy for almost everything.

### Shape, space, motion

- Radius: panel 14, row/button 9, badge 5, pill 999. Nothing else.
- Spacing on the world side: 6 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 22. Row height 43px.
- Motion: animate `transform` and `opacity` only. Never `transition: all`.
  Easing `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` — never bounce, never elastic.
  150–250ms micro, 400–600ms moments, 800ms+ only for arrival. The arrival
  waits 900ms after the world is ready, then travels to the student's place.
  A short hop glides there in under a second. A long one freezes the frame,
  jumps the camera to just before the stop, and dissolves (700ms) into a
  1200ms glide. Any input cancels it (`ARRIVE_DELAY_MS`, `LONG_HOP`,
  `DISSOLVE_MS`, `LAND_MS` in `src/components/world-v6/WorldV6.tsx`).
  `prefers-reduced-motion` turns every animation into a state change.
- The camera's smoothing stays at `6.0/s`, delta-time corrected. It is the
  difference between a glide and a snap.

### State iconography

Consistency carries the place-ness. Shapes, not icons from a library:

| Thing | Shape |
|---|---|
| Watch lesson | circle (dot) — filled when done, outlined when not, half-tint when started |
| Milestone (action item) | **diamond** (rotated square), `--color-milestone` |
| Ready to watch | small right-pointing triangle, `--color-accent`. Shown **only** where true. |
| Current area (marker) | the marker's border in `--color-accent`, gentle pulse |
| Locked | outline in `--color-state-locked`, no fill |

**Never mark the negative on every row.** "Not recorded yet" on 17 of 18
rows was measured on real data and carries no information. Mark what *is*
ready; state the count once in the area header; say "not recorded yet" in
the player, where the student actually meets it.

**What the lesson page says under the video.** The player
(`LessonPlayer`) draws a thin progress bar under the video. Three lines of
text can appear under that progress bar, in this order, each in
`--color-ink-2` at the Row size (`.barNote` in
`src/components/world/world-ui.module.css`):

- When the video has said nothing 12 seconds after its frame was put on the page:
  `This video didn't load. Refresh the page. If it keeps happening, tell the team.`
- When the video played to its end and the server's answer to that last
  heartbeat still says the lesson's 95% point is not reached:
  `This lesson finished but didn't register. Tell the team so we can fix it.`
- When the lesson reached its end and was not counted because too little of
  it was played:
  `Not marked as watched yet. You've watched {N}% of this lesson and it needs {M}%. Skipped parts don't count.`

`{N}` is the share of the lesson really played, rounded down. `{M}` is the
share it needs, 80 unless the floor is changed.

The second line means the catalog's length for the lesson is missing or
longer than the real video, so no watching can count it and only the team
can fix it. It goes when a later answer from the server is past the 95%
point, or when the lesson is counted.

A slow or refused video keeps its frame on the page. The first line shows
under the progress bar and goes as soon as the player says anything, so a
video that was slow to load still plays. All three lines are Claude's draft
(2026-10-12) and wait for Lovro and Karlo.

## Two faces, one product

**Student side ships dark** — the world, and everything derived from it
above. No light mode, no theme toggle. It is an experience the student pays
to be inside.

**Admin side ships light** (`/admin/*`, scoped to `.admin-shell`). Warm cream
canvas `#EFEDE6`, white cards, hairline borders, pearl accent `#8E8E84`. The
admin is a tool Lovro and Astrid use to make decisions about real people's
access; operational density and evidence you read carefully work better on a
light face. **Nothing in this file applies to `/admin`.** Admin follows
`src/components/admin/ui.tsx`: one radius (10), spacing 8/16/24/32/48, type
11/13/20/32, colour is signal not decoration.

## Accessibility baseline

- Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. (`--color-ink` on `--color-surface-0` is ~14:1;
  `--color-ink-3` on it is ~4.6:1 — the floor for anything that must be read.)
- Focus ring: 2px solid `--color-ink`, offset 2px, with a dark halo behind
  it (`0 0 0 5px rgba(29,24,38,.92)`), so it clears 3:1 on the pale sky and
  on the dark chrome alike. The 30% ink ring it replaced measured 1.2 to
  2.5:1. `--focus-ring` and `--focus-halo` are declared with the other
  tokens in `src/components/world-v6/tokens.module.css`.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no pulses, no auto-travel tween, no flights.
- Touch targets ≥ 40px.
- Markers are real `<button>`s. In the app a place and a lesson each have a
  real address: opening a place makes it `/world/{areaId}`, and Watch, a panel
  row and the feature row make it `/world/{areaId}/{lessonId}`
  (`src/components/world/WorldMap.tsx`). On the map those controls are buttons
  that change the address; on the lesson page the back and next controls are
  links. Screen readers get "Area — N of M lessons done".

## Design principles

1. **The world is the product.** Every student screen either *is* the world or
   sits over it or (on phones) echoes it. No generic SaaS patterns: no toast
   stacks, no right-side drawers, no chat bubbles, no blue progress bars.
2. **Calm confidence over urgency.** The one deadline in the product (the
   discount) is stated as mono text. Never flashes, never pulses red.
3. **Lit, not glowing — in the interface.** No gradient text, no purple/cyan
   UI palette, no glowing UI except the current-area marker. The world is
   different: its night places glow (see "Glow belongs to the world at night").
4. **Typography does the hierarchy, colour does state.**
5. **Respect the student's time.** One clear next action. No copy that
   restates the title above it. No button that just says "Dismiss".
6. **Dark student, light admin.** Deliberate, not drift.
7. **Plain titles.** Area and lesson names are what the thing is. No metaphor,
   no riddle. The place names ("The Falls", "The Glowing Forest") never reach
   the UI.
8. **Every stop is a screenshot.** If a stop would not be worth sharing, it
   is not finished.

## Bans

- Bouncy / elastic easing.
- Right-side drawer modals for primary content (tried, felt thin, reverted).
- Icon libraries used generically. The shapes above, or nothing.
- Translucent panels anywhere except the one over the world.
- Sync / refresh buttons on the student side. It syncs silently.
- "Let's get started" / "You're awesome!" / "Welcome back, champion!".
- Marking the absence of something on every row.
- Neon, pixel art, mascots, confetti, stickers, trophies.
